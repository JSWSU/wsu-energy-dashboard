The question this page answers: when internal utility rates are rebuilt each fall, what cost basis should they stand on? Eight years of history lets us simulate each option against what actually happened, instead of arguing hypotheticals.

1. Spike inheritance: what each cost basis would have charged

Simulated rule: rates for year N are set from the blended $/MMBTU of the prior 1, 2, or 3 years. The solid line is what cost actually did. A single-year basis tracks best in calm years and passes the FY2023 spike straight into FY2024 rates; smoothing buys stability at the cost of lag. This is a real tradeoff, and now it is a measured one.

3. Rates need fuel-specific series, and we have them

Blended $/MMBTU hides opposite stories: electric cost per unit is steady and tariff-driven, while gas is where every shock lives. Steam and gas rates should ride the gas series; electric rates the tariff series. Monthly splits exist back to FY2015 in the archives.

4. The FY2028 gas basis is largely already contracted

The Kinect hedge book fixes most gas volume at known prices (FY2026 coverage shown below). For the FY2028 rate build this means the gas component can be priced from actual forward contracts plus a quantified swing exposure, instead of extrapolating history.

5. Rollout plan (feeds the October 2026 FY28 rate kickoff)

  1. Keep the locked philosophy, test the basis. FY-2 volumes + FY-1 costs stays the default; bring the 2-yr-basis simulation to Kate and Phil as a measured alternative with this page as the exhibit.
  2. Add a true-up mechanism. Whatever basis is chosen, an explicit annual reconciliation (realized vs charged, rolled into next year) caps the damage of any FY23-style year. Simulate its behavior on this history first.
  3. Split the rate build by fuel. Steam/gas rates from the gas series + hedge-book forward prices; electric rates from the Avista tariff + realized campus $/kWh.
  4. Publish rate adequacy. One standing chart: internal billed rate vs realized cost per unit, updated annually, so drift is visible before it compounds.
  5. Document the seasonality cross-subsidy. Winter months cost 2-3× summer; keep flat annual rates if desired, but record the shape so the choice is deliberate.
