The idea in one sentence: stop forecasting dollars directly; forecast energy volume (which is stable and weather-driven) and price it with numbers that are already known (the Kinect hedge book and the published Avista tariff).

1. The problem: the current method misses when prices move

The official 5-year-average method (reconstructed exactly, 0.00% vs surviving stored projections) worked in stable years and failed badly in the FY2023 gas spike. Averaging history means a price shock takes five years to propagate into the forecast.

2. Evidence: campus energy volume is weather

3. Evidence: the FY2023 miss was price, not usage

4. The price side is largely already known

WSU's gas is bought forward in fixed-price Kinect packages. The FY2026 invoices show most volume was hedged at known prices; only the swing residual floats with the market. Electric prices come from the published Avista tariff. Hedged volume × contract price is arithmetic, not forecasting.

5. Rollout plan

  1. Parallel column first. Add the volume × price forecast beside the current method on the FY2027 page for a full year. No method change until it proves out.
  2. Volume model. Gas volume = regression on 30-year-normal HDD (shown above); electric volume = 3-year monthly average (stable). Re-fit each July.
  3. Gas price. Hedged Dths at contract package prices straight from the Kinect invoices; swing residual at a stated market assumption with the exposure quantified.
  4. Electric price. Approved Avista tariff schedule, not a trend factor.
  5. Error bands. Publish the historical miss range with every forecast (±8% normal years, +40% recorded gas-shock worst case) so leadership sees uncertainty.
  6. Monthly true-up. As bills post, show variance split into weather, volume, and price components using the HDD regression, so every miss has a named cause.
